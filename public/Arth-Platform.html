Lead difficulty

Hard leads are worth more points · visible to managers only
New, in scope
THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES Executives claim the hot leads and let the cold ones rot. Every points system in sales has this failure, and no amount of monitoring fixes it, the incentive is simply wrong.

If a cold lead is worth three times a hot one, working cold leads becomes the rational choice. The dealer's conversion rate on cold and warm improves, which is where the unclaimed revenue in a dealership actually sits.

Four bands, computed by the system

BandTypicallyMultiplierA test drive is worthShare of enquiries

Two rules that make it safe

1 · The system computes it. No manager can set it. If a manager could, managers would inflate their own team's scores, and within two months the number would mean nothing.

2 · It locks at assignment and never moves. If difficulty could be revised after the outcome is known, it becomes a way to explain away failure, "that one was very cold" said after losing it.

Delivery chain

Specification for Phase 5. The operational screen is separate.
Being built now
WHY THIS DOCUMENT EXISTS The product currently stops at the booking. Booking carries no promised delivery date at all, and there is no promise history anywhere. So the delivery screen in this prototype is a picture of something that cannot be built from what exists.

This is the same screen restated as a specification, because the delivery chain is what Phase 5 is building first. Everything below is either a rule the dev team can code from or a line saying nothing records it yet.

1. The promised date is an event, never a field

This is the one decision that cannot be revisited later. If the promised delivery date ships as a mutable column on Booking, three things become permanently unbuildable and no amount of work afterwards recovers them.

What breaksWhy
The Cockpit's promise-moved-twice cardYou cannot count movements of a value that only holds its current state
Delivery promise accuracy by branchPromised against actual needs the promise as it stood at booking, not as it stands now
Who told the customer the 12thThe single most asked question in a delivery dispute. A field cannot answer it
Required shape. A DeliveryPromise row per promise, append-only, carrying: the date promised, who set it, whether the person set it or the critical path computed it, and the reason if a person overrode the computation. The current promise is the latest row. Same pattern as ExceptionEvent, PointEntry and the lead ledger, so no new concept.
And the computed date is the default, not the entry. A salesperson may override it, and the override is recorded with a reason. What must never exist is a date typed with nothing behind it, which is what every dealership has today.
